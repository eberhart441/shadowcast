# 09 - Prior art and competitive landscape for Shadowcast

Research date: 2026-11-11. Scope: academic, open-source, commercial, first-responder/military, patents.
Conventions: every claim carries a URL. **[secondary]** means the claim comes from press, a search
snippet, a forum or an aggregator rather than the primary document. **[memory]** means it is from
background knowledge and was not re-checked in this session. **[not checked]** marks known gaps.
None of this is legal advice.

## Verdict up front

The **core concept has been done, published and even commercialised.** The University of Utah
built and published it in 2009-2013: radio nodes placed around a building, per-link variance of
the received signal, a tomographic heatmap, Kalman tracking, no per-site training, through-wall
results, firefighters and police named as users. Its spin-off Xandem pitched it to the **same
tactical "throw nodes around the building" customer** in a 2011-12 NSF SBIR. The planned redo's
radio protocol (every node a transceiver, taking turns, piggybacking its measurements of the
others in its own broadcast) is Utah's *Spin/multi-Spin* token-passing protocol. A 2025
undergraduate thesis has **already ported that protocol to ESP32 + ESP-NOW + CSI amplitude**.

What has *not* been done, as far as this search found: a **validated, ruggedised, rapid-deploy,
training-free CSI-tomography kit on commodity WiFi silicon at 16-32 nodes, built and evaluated
for fire and tactical entry**. The novelty is in engineering, validation and product, not in the
idea. Expect weak patentability for the concept and some freedom-to-operate (FTO) exposure from
the WiFi-motion patent holders (Cognitive Systems; Origin Wireless, which ADT bought in 2026).

## 2. Academic RTI / device-free localisation

**Utah SPAN lab (Patwari) is the direct ancestor of Shadowcast.**
- *RTI with wireless networks* (Wilson & Patwari, IEEE TMC 9(5), 2010): attenuation RTI. Press reports a 28-node network around a square of indoor atrium and of lawn [secondary]. https://span.ece.utah.edu/uploads/RTI.pdf, https://newatlas.com/radio-tomographic-imaging/13092/
- *VRTI* (arXiv 0909.5417, journal "See-Through Walls", 2011): uses the **variance** of link RSS, so it images *motion*, not static attenuation. This is exactly the Shadowcast idea, with RSS where we use CSI amplitude. 34 nodes at 2.4 GHz (802.15.4) around four walls of a home, about 780 ft²: 3.37 ft average tracking error and 1.46 ft spot error, with a Kalman filter, and no site-specific calibration. The paper's motivating scenario is responders who "deploy radio sensors around ... the building ... by throwing or launching them". https://arxiv.org/abs/0909.5417
- *Spin / multi-Spin* (Bocca, Kaltiokallio, Patwari): a token-passing TDMA in which "each device broadcasts packets and stores the RSS of the packets received from all the other devices". Multi-Spin adds channel hopping, on TI CC253x. **This is our planned all-transceiver TDMA with piggybacked features.** https://github.com/SPAN-WashU/radio-tomography, https://theses.liacs.nl/pdf/2013-2014TimvanderMeij.pdf, https://span.ece.utah.edu/products.html
- *Multiple target tracking with RF sensor networks* (Bocca et al., IEEE TMC 2014): tracks up to 4 people in real time, 0.45-0.55 m average error, with Gaussian filtering, clustering and Kalman filtering, in an open room, an apartment and an office. https://arxiv.org/abs/1302.4720
- *Channel diversity / fade-level RTI* (Kaltiokallio et al., MASS 2012; multi-scale model arXiv 1302.5914): 0.10 m average error, and claims the first attenuation-based through-wall RTI. Picking good channels by fade level is the RSS-era equivalent of our subcarrier selection. https://typeset.io/pdf/enhancing-the-accuracy-of-radio-tomographic-imaging-using-4wqgx4619v.pdf, https://arxiv.org/abs/1302.5914
- *RTI + kernel distance* (Zhao, Patwari, Phillips, Venkatasubramanian, IPSN 2013): locates stationary or moving people "without calibration" and cites firefighters locating victims. https://www-old.cs.utah.edu/~jeffp/papers/RTI+KD-ipsn13.pdf
- *Breathfinding* (Patwari et al., 2013/2014): locates a *breathing, motionless* person from RSS alone. This is relevant to the "still person in the room" failure mode. https://arxiv.org/abs/1302.3820; patent US9443419 https://patents.google.com/patent/US9443419
- *Kaltiokallio's later work*: recursive Bayesian filters (EKF and fixed-lag smoothing) for RSS device-free localisation (DFL), IPIN 2018. https://acris.aalto.fi/ws/portalfiles/portal/31241798/ELEC_Kaltokallio_et_al_Recursive_Bayesian_Filters_IPIN2018.pdf. Work after 2018: **not checked**.
- *Through-wall localisation with moving transceivers* (Hillyard, Maas, Patwari, Kasera 2015): UWB-IR radios moved outside the room, 0.25 m average error. It argues that responders may not have time to deploy many fixed nodes, which is a direct counter-argument to our deployment model. https://arxiv.org/abs/1511.06703

**Other device-free localisation**
- Youssef, Mah, Agrawala, *Device-free passive localisation*, MobiCom 2007: founded the field and targets intrusion detection. https://www.csd.uoc.gr/~hy439/reading/list_2011/10.1.1.112.6849.pdf
- USNA roadside RTI (Anderson et al., IEEE JSTSP 2014): Zigbee RTI for vehicle surveillance, by the military-academy community. https://arxiv.org/abs/1701.00691
- LiFS (MobiCom 2016): model-based **CSI** DFL with no offline training on COTS WiFi; median 0.5 m LOS and 1.1 m NLOS. This is the closest academic "CSI + multi-link + no training" result. https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/1527423/1/LiFS%20Final%20Submission%20Manuscript.pdf
- CSI-based RTI: Xu, Liu, Gao 2018, "Enhanced RTI with CSI-MIMO measurements" (abstract only seen) https://pure.bit.edu.cn/en/publications/an-enhanced-radio-tomographic-imaging-with-csi-mimo-measurements/; Rodrigo 2019 (Colombo), a linear-model CSI-RTI student report https://dl.ucsc.cmb.ac.lk/jspui/bitstream/123456789/4796/1/2019%20CS%20146.pdf; Kim et al. 2025, SDR multi-link MIMO DFL https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2505.04085.
- CSI activity recognition (trained, single-link, mostly not localisation): E-eyes (MobiCom 2014), CARM (MobiCom 2015), Widar3.0 (MobiSys 2019, 92.7% in-domain and 82.6-92.4% cross-domain without retraining). URLs are in the table. Widar 1/2 and WiDeo: **[memory]**, not re-checked.
- MIT CSAIL (Katabi): Wi-Vi (SIGCOMM 2013) is MIMO nulling on USRP and sees movers behind walls with one device. WiTrack (NSDI 2014) is FMCW with a median 10-13 cm in x/y. RF-Pose (CVPR 2018) is camera-supervised, with through-wall AP 58.1. RF-Capture (2015): **[memory]**. All of these are *single-device radar-like* systems, not distributed links. They are the academic case that one active box can beat a node network.
- Surveys: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2111.07038 (10 years of commodity WiFi sensing) and https://arxiv.org/pdf/1901.00715. The second notes that through-wall WiFi is useful to firefighters but that "some earlier systems need many communication nodes".

**Positioning:** every fielded competitor is **a single active radar box at the wall**, priced from thousands to tens of thousands of dollars, with seconds of setup, ITAR or LE-only sales, and an established procurement channel (NIJ testing, FCC certification for LE use). Our differences are cost per node, coverage of a whole room or floor from several sides, a persistent unattended watch while the team stages, and a shared dashboard. Our weaknesses are setup time (placing 16-32 nodes on several sides, often impossible from the inside of a fire), the need for access to more than one side of the room, and stationary-person detection.

## 7. What's novel vs what's been done

**Already done (do not claim as new):**
- Distributed link-based motion imaging (RTI/VRTI), Kalman tracking, multi-person tracking, through-wall, no per-site training (Utah 2009-2014).
- The rapid-deploy-around-a-building pitch for police, fire and military, including GPS self-localising thrown nodes (VRTI paper; Xandem NSF SBIR 2012).
- An all-transceiver round-robin broadcast with piggybacked measurements (Spin/multi-Spin), including on ESP32 + ESP-NOW + CSI (Weerasiri 2025).
- CSI instead of RSS for RTI (Xu 2018; Colombo 2019/2025); calibration-free CSI DFL (LiFS 2016).
- Training-free ESP32 CSI motion scores (ESPectre, esp-radar) and multi-node ESP32 CSI meshes with SAR marketing (RuView).
- Link-disturbance motion sensing in mass-market products (Cognitive, Origin, WiZ, Hue).

**Genuinely underserved / plausibly novel in combination:**
1. **A validated, training-free, CSI-amplitude VRTI on $5 commodity WiFi SoCs at 16-32 nodes and 20-40 Hz per link.** Utah used 802.15.4 RSS; the ESP32 work so far is either trained (thesis, fingerprints) or single-link (ESPectre). Subcarrier diversity standing in for multi-Spin's channel hopping, on WiFi silicon, is a credible engineering contribution if measured.
2. **On-node feature extraction piggybacked in the TDMA beacon.** This removes the payload limit that capped the thesis at a few nodes. It is a small, real systems delta.
3. **A self-adaptive median/MAD baseline that needs no empty-room capture.** This is useful because responders cannot guarantee an empty room, but it is conceptually close to the RTI+KD "without calibration" work and to Utah's online baselines. Treat it as an engineering choice, not an invention.
4. **The productised rapid-deploy kit:** 50 mm ruggedised battery nodes, auto-discovery and placement assistance, a shared web dashboard, honest "moving / not moving / unknown" output, and published through-wall test data on real construction. Nobody has shipped this at this price point. Xandem tried in 2012 and stalled.
5. **Price disruption** against $6k-$30k+ handheld radars. The NSF record put existing through-wall radar at about $100k [secondary, 2011].

## 8. Lessons from those who tried

- **Xandem (Utah spin-off, about 2008-present):** the technology worked well enough to sell TMD kits ($600-$2,200, later $695-$1,395, covering 500-3,000 ft²) through security integrators, for warehouse theft and a Dubai villa. It raised only about $790K in total (mostly NSF/SBIR grants; last round about 13 years ago [secondary]). It ran an Indiegogo consumer push (Xandem Home), then retreated to **licensing "RF Tomography" as a software add-on to other people's wireless products**. Its site still carries a 2025 copyright but lists no products and no first-responder mention. The founder called security "the lowest use of our technology". The tactical through-building SBIR (Phase I, $150k) appears never to have become a product. **Lesson:** a separate node kit is a hard sell, and the value moved to software on existing radios. The tactical market did not pull the product even with a working Phase I. https://www.autm.net/about-tech-transfer/better-world-project/bwp-stories/xandem-radio-tomography, https://securitysystemsnews.com/article/xandem-tmd-sensing-technology-sees-through-walls, https://grantome.com/grant/NSF/IIP-1142493, https://www.cbinsights.com/company/xandem-technology [secondary], https://www.xandem.com
- **Linksys Aware:** a subscription motion feature on routers, shut down in 2024 [secondary]. Consumers would not pay monthly for "WiFi motion".
- **Origin Wireless:** about 13 years of ISP and OEM licensing ended in a $170M ADT acquisition, driven largely by **patents and distribution**. Lesson: in this field the patent estate and channel are the assets; ADT now controls a big block of WiFi-sensing IP.
- **Cognitive Systems:** won by riding chipset SDKs (Qualcomm, Broadcom) and ISPs, not by selling hardware.
- **Utah/academia:** repeated results are good in lab and home settings, but they depend on node density. Hillyard et al. explicitly argue that responders lack time to deploy many fixed nodes. RF-Pose and Wi-Vi show that one active device can see through walls without a network. Expect evaluators to ask "why not just buy a Range-R or a Lumineye?"
- **The thesis's cross-session collapse (96% to 33-46%)** is fresh evidence that trained CSI localisation does not survive a new session. This supports keeping Shadowcast training-free (consistent with the team's decision to reject the trained locator; see memory note `locator-rejected.md`).
- **First-responder procurement:** fielded TTWS goes through NIJ market surveys and T&E, FCC certification for LE/first-responder use, and sometimes ITAR. A product plan must budget for FCC certification (intentional radiator; the ESP32-C5 module certification helps), NIJ-style testing, and ruggedisation.
